    When to go

    The one idea it rests on

    eBird lets a birder mark a checklist complete: a declaration that they listed every species they could identify. On a complete checklist, a species that does not appear is evidence that it was not detected. It is not missing data. It is a verified zero.

    That zero is what makes a probability computable. A pile of sightings gives you a numerator. Complete checklists give you the denominator: of all the complete lists submitted at a hotspot in a given week, how many contained a Wood Thrush?

    Every downstream decision protects that zero. Only complete checklists enter the fit, and the model predicts what a birder would report, which is the right target for someone deciding whether to get up at five.

    How a checklist becomes a number on this map

    Scraping lags seven days. eBird submissions trail the outing; a weekend trip gets entered on Monday. Scraping fresh captures only the people who log in the field, a biased sample.

    Raw records stay private. eBird data is not redistributable, so the checklist database lives in a private Hugging Face dataset. Only fitted coefficients are published, and that public copy is what this page reads, with no token and no server.

    Serving needs no Python. The fitted model is a lookup table plus a sum. This page loads one species' coefficients (about 10 KB), fetches the forecast, and scores every hotspot in your browser. The coefficients update themselves after each monthly refit, and the forecast moves the map every day.

    The model, in plain words

    One ridge (L2-penalised) logistic regression per species, estimating P(reported | hour, week, site, effort, weather).

    BlockTermsWhat it absorbs
    Effortlog duration, log distance, observers, travellingHow hard you looked. The largest effect in the data.
    Time of dayone term per hour, 06:00–20:00Dawn chorus, midday lull, species by species.
    Seasonone term per week of the yearMigration, breeding song, winter absence.

    Weatherwind, temperature, precipitation, cloudThe thing actually under test.

    Why effort has to be in it

    Rain makes birders cut a walk short and makes birds quieter. Leave duration out and the rain coefficient absorbs both: you would publish "rain lowers your chances" when half of that is "rain shortened your walk". Conditioning on effort separates the two.

    Why ridge rather than plain logistic regression

    Some hotspots have only a handful of visits. Unpenalised, a site where the bird was seen on every one of four visits gets an infinite coefficient. Ridge shrinks small-sample sites toward the pooled average and lets a site with hundreds of visits keep its own estimate. The cost is inference: ridge coefficients are biased on purpose, so there are no valid p-values here. The app needs predictions, not significance tests.

    Does it work?

    Each dot is one decile of one species on held-out checklists. On the dashed line, a predicted 40% came true 40% of the time.

    Calibration matters more than ranking here. AUC asks whether the model ranks outings correctly. Calibration asks whether its 40% really means 40%. A number that is never honest about magnitude is no use to someone deciding whether an early start is worth it.

    Never a random split. Checklists from the same trip would leak across it. The model is tested two ways: held out by date (a known hotspot on a day it has not seen, which is what the app does) and held out by site (a place it has never seen). By-date scoring higher than by-site is evidence that site effects carry real information.

    Engineering decisions worth defending

    Weeks by day of year, not date

    Week 31 is week 31 in any year, so last August's checklists fill this August's seasonal term. That is what makes a backfill of historical dates useful, and why the model can speak to a week it has not lived through yet.

    Pinned numerics, reproducible to 1e-7

    numpy and scipy are pinned even though nothing imports them directly. The same data fitted on an arm64 laptop and an x86_64 GitHub runner agreed across 17,526 terms to a maximum difference of 7.9e-08. That only holds because both install the same versions.

    Merge, never overwrite

    A laptop and CI both write to the shared database. Every table has a natural key, so a sync inserts missing rows and leaves existing ones alone. A push that would shrink the published data is refused outright, because eBird rows for a day that has aged out cannot be re-fetched.

    A missing week is not an average week

    Scoring a week with no fitted term as zero would silently remove seasonality, which is most of what the model knows about migrants. The app and this page grey those cells out and say why, rather than showing a confident wrong number.

    Incident · 2026-09-22 to 09-27

    The pipeline filled its own storage

    Every push stored the whole database as a new version, and Hugging Face kept all of them: over 1,900 copies, 99.97 GB, against a 100 GB cap. For five days each run scraped for hours and then failed at the upload. Squashing the history alone freed nothing; the orphaned objects had to be deleted explicitly. The push now does both once history passes 20 commits, and storage sits at about 60 MB.

    Scored in your browser, verified against the source

    This page runs a JavaScript port of the app's TypeScript scoring layer. Before publishing, the two were compared on every species at several dates, hours and hotspots: identical to the last bit.

    Honest limits

    • Detection, not presence. It predicts whether you would report a bird, not whether one is there. Right for a birder; wrong if read ecologically.
    • A sample of hotspots, not the state. The model covers the busiest public hotspots. Building a denominator costs API calls per checklist, so coverage is bounded by scraping budget, not by design.
    • Effort is endogenous. Birders extend a good outing, so duration is partly a consequence of success. The effort effect is inflated by an unknown amount.
    • Weather is coarse. Training weather sits on a reanalysis grid tens of kilometres across, which attenuates its coefficients toward zero. On this page the forecast is also shared across each 0.25° cell, and forecast error passes straight into the number.
    • Observers self-select on weather. Nobody birds in a storm, so the observed weather range is truncated and whoever does go out is unusual.
    • The eBird feed truncates any county-day above 200 checklists, under-sampling exactly the busiest spring mornings.
    • Calibration is out-of-sample by scrape time, not out-of-time. The backfill collects historical days, so it tests generalisation to unseen sites and days rather than drift since the fit.
    • Map colours are relative. They stretch from the lowest to the highest hotspot at the chosen time, so a dark dot for a rare bird can still be a small probability. Hover for the number.
