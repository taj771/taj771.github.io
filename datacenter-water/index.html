Can a U.S. River Basin Supply a Data Center?

A national freshwater supply assessment for U.S. data center siting, combining USGS National Water Availability Assessment data across 83,000+ HUC12 watersheds with an inventory of 3,609 operating and planned facilities. For every HUC8 basin, the pipeline answers four water stress questions under greenfield, cumulative, and plus-one scenarios.

Q3

Q4
How much of that public supply already comes from groundwater?
Method note. Each analysis runs under three scenarios — Greenfield (one new facility against basin supply), Cumulative (all existing facilities), and Plus-one (cumulative plus one more) — and at three demand rates: 2.17, 5.55, and 21.08 million gallons per month. HUC12-to-HUC8 aggregation uses outlet routing on the USGS Watershed Boundary Dataset flow network, so routed availability variables (depth in mm) are read at the basin outlet and converted to volume rather than summed across sub-watersheds.

Where the facilities are

3,609 data center facilities (operating and planned) in the contiguous United States, by development stage. Facilities cluster in Northern Virginia, Texas, Arizona, Illinois, and California — regions that account for the majority of the national water stress risk.

Key findings

  • A greenfield siting screen — testing a single new facility against basin supply — misses the cumulative stress already present. Northern Virginia's Ashburn basin (02070008) passes a greenfield test across all 129 months but holds roughly 8% of all U.S. data centers.
  • Data center burden on public water supply ranges across three orders of magnitude — from under 1% to roughly 2,000% of host basin supply — depending on facility concentration and basin hydrology.
  • Stress under large-campus demand rates (21 MGM) is materially higher than under mid-range rates in arid western basins and parts of the Southeast, suggesting that cooling technology choice is a first-order siting variable.
  • Facilities that buy from public water systems are invisible in USGS withdrawal dockets — they appear under public supply, not industrial. The Q3 index surfaces this hidden demand by attributing public-supply deliveries back to the facilities drawing them.
